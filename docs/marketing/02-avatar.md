## Avatar 1 · The coach: "Sam, the coach who outgrew the spreadsheet"

**In one line:** an online or hybrid coach with 10 to 60 clients, who is a better coach than an administrator, spends evenings and Sundays on check-ins, and is quietly afraid that a client will feel unseen and leave.

### 🔍 Demographic & general information
- **Age range:** 25 to 45; the core is 28 to 40. (58% of trainers in PTDC's survey were 25–34; the highest earners 35–44.)
- **Gender:** leans male (about two thirds to three quarters of trainers), with a large female-led segment in fat-loss, nutrition and post-natal coaching. Imagery and copy must not assume a male bodybuilder.
- **Location:** English-speaking first: UK and Ireland, US, Australia, Canada; then the Netherlands and the Nordics (high English, high coaching prices). Euro and pound pricing matters; several coaches complain about USD-only billing.
- **Monthly revenue:** roughly **€2,000 to €12,000 a month** (10 to 60 clients at €130–€250 each); a typical middle of €4,000–€6,000. The median coach on one platform has only 5 clients, so ours is already in the top fifth.
- **Professional backgrounds:** gym-floor PT who went online in or after 2020; former athlete, bodybuilder or physique competitor; a client transformed by their own coach who became a coach (Finlay's lineage); nutritionist or physio adding training. Usually several certifications (Precision Nutrition, NASM/ACE/NSCA), often a sports-science degree.
- **Typical identities:** self-employed, a one-person brand on Instagram, trains hard themselves, reads evidence-based content, proud of individual programming. Sees themselves as a coach, not a salesperson or an admin. Hybrid coaches split the day between a gym and their phone.

### 🚩 Key challenges & pain points
1. **They can't see every client properly any more**
   - Programmes, logs, check-ins, photos and food live in five places (Sheet, Drive, Docs, WhatsApp, MyFitnessPal); "the coaching conversation lives in three places."
   - Spotting progress or a stall means reading weights, reps and RPE row by row, sheet by sheet, client by client, and building charts by hand.
   - Clients ask "which programme am I on?" because the version in their inbox is out of date; form-check videos are lost somewhere in two months of WhatsApp.
2. **Admin has eaten their evenings and their boundaries**
   - Check-in day is a marathon ("Tuesday-night check-in marathon"); the business only grows by adding more hours.
   - Messages arrive on their personal phone at all hours ("20 people messaging her 24 7").
   - The tools they tried were meant to fix this but added clicks: "Too many button clicks to enter workout results."
3. **They can't trust the tools they've tried**
   - Bugs that lose a client's data at the worst moment: "Closes out, wipes the set history, then tells them they worked out for 60 seconds."
   - Support that answers with templates since the big apps were bought by investment firms; price cliffs and paid add-ons ("charging an extra 50 dollars for automation is ridiculous").
   - Coach phone apps that don't really work ("Coach app is next to useless"), so they're tied to a laptop.

### 🌟 Goals & aspirations
- **Short-term goals:**
  - Get check-in day under control and stop working Sunday evenings.
  - Stop losing data and videos; have one place they trust.
  - Take on 5 to 10 more clients without adding hours, and look professional doing it.
- **Long-term aspirations:**
  - A known name in their niche with a waiting list; 50 to 100 clients, or a small team of coaches.
  - Income that doesn't depend on hours on a gym floor; "the time, money, and freedom to be with my family."
  - Clients who stay for years and refer friends (retention, not just acquisition).

### 🧠 Emotional drivers & psychological insights
- **Pride in being a real coach.** They believe individual attention is what they sell; anything that makes them feel like a template-shifter or an "app coach" hurts their identity.
- **Fear of being found out as stretched thin.** The day a client feels unseen, they lose them, and they know clients can tell ("No one there even knows who I am" is what their clients write about other coaches).
- **Switching fatigue and distrust.** Many have been burned by one platform already; trust and quick human support matter more than another feature list.

### 💬 General direct client quotes (here: the coach's own words)
- "Trainerize centralized all my programming, macro tracking, and client check-ins"
- "It keeps my clients on task for tracking and accountability."
- "Highly recommend it for any trainers or small gyms looking for a simple platform to run their business."

### 🚩 Pain points & frustrations
- "I am getting glitches all the time when creating plans for my clients."
- "Comments are virtually inaccessible after you leave them"
- "Too many button clicks to enter workout results."
- "It worked at five clients. It worked at ten. Somewhere between ten and twenty, it stopped working." (a vendor's description of the coach's story, not a coach's own words)

### 🎯 Mindset quotes
- "It is about you can't help people if you burn out."
- "There's no painless way to scale once you're in the 20-30 hours of PT range."
- "And if people love what you do, they will pay more."

### 🗣 Quotes on emotional state & personal drivers
- "I was a struggling trainer.."
- "And I'm working at strange hours that maybe disrupt your family."
- "Do I charge crazy high prices, do I take on more clients and burn myself out."

### 📢 Quotes on emotional responses to struggles
- "For me, this is the final straw."
- "I've been a client for almost a decade…but not much longer."
- "I felt that my experience was dismissed rather than acknowledged"
- "I'm tired of being an unpaid therapist for energy-draining clients."

### 🚀 Quotes on motivation & urgency around success
- "I've already started transferring clients over to everfit."
- "I have better client retention than I did before using Everfit"
- "Now I have the time, money, and freedom to be with my family and to do what I truly love to do."

### 🚩 Key emotional fears & deep frustrations
- **Losing a paying client because something broke or because they didn't notice:** "I lost a paying client—$2,200 AUD gone." / "We have had multiple clients leave because of how bad this app is"
- **Being one more coach who "says the same thing every week"**, with clients spotting automated or AI-sounding replies.
- **Burning out and leaving the job they love:** "If you burn out and leave the industry, you are not helping people in this industry at all anymore."
- **Switching again and it going wrong:** moving 40 clients, losing history, a new app that breaks mid-workout.

### 🧠 Emotional & psychographic insights
- They judge a tool by what their clients say about it ("my clients just buy in"); the client's experience is the coach's reputation.
- They tolerate admin as "the job" until it visibly costs them a client or a weekend; that moment is when they buy. **[Inference]**
- They are not technical and are impatient with friction; they praise what is "simple", "no fuss", "doesn't make my brain hurt".
- They respect evidence and numbers (strength trends, PBs, adherence) but don't want to build the charts themselves.

### 📌 Typical emotional journey
- **Awareness:** "It worked at five clients. It worked at ten." Growth that felt like success starts to feel heavy; Sunday evenings disappear into check-ins.
- **Frustration:** scrolling WhatsApp for week three's form video, a client asking which programme is current, missing that a client has stalled for three weeks, replying late and feeling guilty.
- **Desperation & seeking solutions:** tries Trainerize, TrueCoach or Everfit; finds it clunky, buggy or expensive, or loses a client to a bug; considers going back to Excel; asks peers what they use.
- **Relief & commitment:** finds one place that is fast and reliable, shows them who needs them today, makes clients feel properly coached, and is run by people who answer. Commits once they see their own clients using it without complaint.

## Avatar 2 · The coach's client: "the client who wants to be seen" (secondary)

Short, because the client doesn't buy NorCoach; but the coach buys to keep this person. Useful for the App Store listing and for coaches introducing the app to their clients.

- **Who:** pays a coach €130–€250 a month; trains 3 to 5 times a week; wants results and accountability; logs on their phone between sets. (The founder is one.)
- **Core pain:** feeling unseen. "No one there even knows who I am." / "Feels like they say the same thing every week during check in." / "She didn't keep tabs on my progression as much as she should have."
- **Tool pain:** "it is the worst app I have used in terms of workout tracking." / "I'd like an easier way to track sets and reps, charts that clearly show strength improvements over time." / spreadsheets on a phone mid-set.
- **What they love:** "I finally feel like I've got someone in my corner who I can be accountable to" / a coach who asks what is and isn't working / feedback on their own lift.
- **What they fear:** paying for coaching that turns out to be a template and automated replies ("felt automated and AI-generated").
- **[Inference] What wins them:** proof the coach looked: a reply that names their numbers, a programme that changes when they stall, a video of their own lift with the coach's drawing and voice over it.

## What this sets up for the offer brief

- **The emotional core:** coaches don't buy software to save time; they buy it so no client ever feels unseen, without giving up their evenings.
- **The founder's story fits exactly:** the client who could feel his coach couldn't keep up, and the coach running it all in Google Sheets, sheet by sheet.
- **Must be answered head-on:** trust (new tool, who's behind it, will anyone answer), switching pain (moving clients, data), and "will my clients use it".
